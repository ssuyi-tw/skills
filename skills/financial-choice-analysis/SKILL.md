# Financial Choice and Exchange Analysis

## Purpose

Help the user see a financial choice clearly before deciding: what they believe they are exchanging, what the exchange actually delivers, why it attracts them, and how it changes their future position and behavior.

Treat choice as an exchange of capabilities, claims, resources, and foregone alternatives. This is an analytical lens, not a claim that every choice has a market counterparty or that every value can be priced in money. Time with family, peace of mind, autonomy, trust, and attention may be legitimate ends. Do not silently replace these ends with return maximization.

Use three connected layers:
1. Objective exchange: rights, actors, mechanisms, price, exposure, and opportunity cost.
2. Subjective valuation: needs, beliefs, framing, emotions, and possible behavioral mechanisms.
3. Behavioral consequences: how the choice changes future constraints, attention, incentives, and available choices.

The economic-position model supplies the external causal structure; psychological analysis explains how the user perceives and chooses that position. Neither alone determines what they should value.

## When to Use

Trigger when the user wants to understand:
- what buying, holding, selling, borrowing, insuring, or retaining cash really exchanges;
- why a financial option feels attractive or difficult to relinquish;
- whether a decision serves a real need, relieves immediate discomfort, or both;
- how rights, control, pricing power, exposure, and opportunity costs shape outcomes;
- how a choice affects future freedom, resilience, family time, attention, or behavior.

Examples: "What does holding this mean?", "What am I exchanging?", "Why do I want this?", "Am I holding because it remains worthwhile or because I want to break even?", "What does this do to my future options?"

Do not trigger solely because a ticker or asset class appears. Price lookup, bookkeeping, return calculation, or order execution does not require this framework unless the user asks for mechanisms or tradeoffs. Do not conduct unsolicited psychological audits of ordinary choices.

For allocation, position sizing, tax, insurance adequacy, leverage analysis, or specific product evaluation, use a suitable financial-planning workflow; load personal-finance-planning if available. That companion skill is optional and is not bundled here. If unavailable, establish cash purposes, obligations, risk capacity, and authoritative product or tax evidence before giving specific recommendations. Reuse known relevant context before asking for it again. Do not expand conceptual discussion into a household plan or transaction.

## Workflow

Use the layers selectively; do not require every response to display a worksheet. Lead with the main causal judgment, connect it to a concrete case, and distinguish what is known from what is hypothesized.

### 0. Establish the decision and the desired effect

Identify the actual choice, including keeping the current position, waiting, or choosing a different instrument. Recover the user's stated purpose and what a successful outcome would change in their life. Distinguish an exploration from an immediate decision.

Use known context; ask only for missing facts that could change the analysis. A missing product contract limits product conclusions, while a missing personal motive calls for a tentative hypothesis or a targeted question, not an invented diagnosis.

Completion: the choice, next-best alternative, intended benefit, and material unknowns are explicit. If the alternative is unknown, mark it unresolved rather than presuming an index fund or maximum-return objective.

### 1. Objective exchange: what changes economically?

#### Rights and obligations

Identify what is held: direct ownership, creditor claim, residual equity, fund share, contractual protection, or contingent borrowing access. Separate underlying asset, issuer, custodian, intermediary, and wrapper.

Ask who owes or grants what to whom, in which currency, when, under what conditions, and with what priority and enforcement. Include distributions, voting, redemption, transfer, collateral, termination, and dilution where material. State what the holder can and cannot demand. Specific rights require the actual contract and jurisdiction.

#### Actors, control, and value capture

Separate ownership, operational control, information, and enforcement. Identify whose decisions determine the result and where incentives diverge. Trace productive activity or contractual performance to holder benefit; creating value is not the same as capturing it. Customers, workers, suppliers, government, creditors, management, and owners can receive different shares.

Explain who can reinvest resources, change prices, reset funding terms, issue claims, restrict exit, or force liquidation. Corporate pricing power is conditional and differs from sovereign monetary authority; neither guarantees shareholder returns or purchasing power.

#### Exposure as a cross-layer check

Exposure is sensitivity to changes, not an additional type of ownership and not inherently harmful. Trace:

> Change in conditions -> actor response -> contractual or operating transmission -> effect on cash flow, wealth, or available choices.

For material exposures, identify direction, timing, thresholds, nonlinear effects, and ability to exit where evidence supports them. Separate operating effects from market repricing and price volatility from cash-flow solvency. Risk additionally depends on adverse scenarios, severity, and capacity to endure them.

Consider net exposure across assets, liabilities, income, and future needs. A dollar asset can offset a dollar obligation; employer stock can compound salary dependence; near-term debt can turn a temporary price decline into forced selling. Do not infer resilience from a long intended holding period alone.

#### Exchange, price, and alternatives

Separate primary issuance from secondary transfers and say where purchase money goes. Map what is relinquished and obtained: liquidity, money, time, flexibility, control, future claims, protection, upside, and obligations.

Parties can trade because of different beliefs, alternatives, liabilities, horizons, risk capacity, or institutional constraints. Identical forecasts can still support exchange. Do not assume one side is wrong, every trade benefits both sides, or unobserved motives are known.

Keep three questions distinct: what benefits the right can deliver; what the price already assumes; and whether it improves this holder's position relative to their next-best option. Diminishing marginal usefulness of cash creates room for exchange, not proof that any asset is attractive.

Completion: state the exchanged capabilities, causal path to benefit, meaningful failure scenario, and relevant alternative. Without valuation inputs, do not invent allocations or price targets.

### 2. Subjective valuation: why is this exchange attractive?

Separate stated purpose, observed behavior, and psychological hypothesis. Explore only mechanisms relevant to the puzzle: security, autonomy, fear of missing out, loss aversion, reference prices, regret, action bias, avoidance, sunk costs, mental accounting, social obligation, or delegated judgment.

Do not equate emotion with irrationality. Peace of mind, trust, relationships, and simplicity can be real benefits. Ask whether the option reliably supplies that benefit, at what cost, and whether a better alternative supplies it. Purpose-specific mental accounts can protect commitments rather than merely distort choices.

Use a targeted counterfactual where helpful:
- If I did not already own it, would I choose it today under the same circumstances? Account for taxes, transaction costs, and other genuine switching costs.
- If my purchase price were hidden, what evidence would still justify holding?
- If the same terms came from someone else, would their appeal change? Preserve any real difference in service or trust.
- If the current discomfort disappeared, would the exchange still serve my goal?
- What evidence would make me decline, reduce, or reverse this choice?

These are probes, not diagnostic tests. Offer competing explanations and ask a discriminating question only if it changes the judgment. Do not claim access to unconscious motives, diagnose a person, or invent psychological precision from one message. Immediate emotional relief and durable economic benefit can coexist.

Completion: label the stated need, plausible mechanism, supporting evidence, and what would distinguish alternatives. It is valid to conclude that motives remain unresolved.

### 3. Behavioral consequences: what does the choice do to future me?

Trace how the choice changes liquidity, required payments, reversibility, attention, habits, incentives, and future options. Consider ordinary and adverse conditions, not merely expected returns.

Examples:
- Capital lockup can reduce ability to refuse work or spend time with family.
- Cash can preserve flexibility or enable indefinite postponement, depending on purpose and rules.
- A concentrated holding can increase monitoring and incentives to seek confirming evidence.
- Leverage can convert willingness to wait into a payment deadline or forced-sale threshold.
- Automation or delegation can reduce decision burden while introducing dependence and oversight needs.

State behavioral predictions conditionally; a possibility is not evidence that the user already behaves that way. Identify an observable sign that would confirm or weaken the prediction. Look for a simpler alternative or optional safeguard when appropriate, without prescribing a new operating regime unasked.

Completion: describe a plausible future consequence, its mechanism, and how it could be checked. Explain whether the choice expands or constrains capabilities important to the user's stated goals.

### 4. Synthesize without taking over the decision

Connect the three layers:
> I expect to obtain [benefit]. In substance I exchange [present capability] for [rights and dependencies], absorbing [exposures]. Its appeal may come from [stated need or qualified hypothesis]. It changes future options through [mechanism]. It beats [alternative] only if [condition].

When deciding, recommend a direction only when evidence supports it; otherwise give the decision boundary and unresolved evidence. State what would change the conclusion. The user retains authority over values and consequential choices. Analysis never authorizes a transaction.

## Asset Baselines

Starting models, not confirmed product terms:
- Cash: accepted nominal purchasing power, not fixed real purchasing power. Deposits are bank claims with withdrawal rules and jurisdiction-specific protections. Liquidity includes the option not to sell another asset at a bad time.
- Bonds: contractual payment claims, usually senior to common equity, with instrument-specific default, priority, call, maturity, and enforcement risks. Yield derives from price and assumptions, not a separately guaranteed reward.
- Common equity: residual ownership in an entity; the company owns its assets and shareholders cannot individually withdraw them. Capital allocation, governance, distributions, dilution, value capture, and price paid determine holder outcomes. Ordinary limited liability generally limits losses to invested capital; margin or other obligations add separate exposure.
- Real estate: location-specific use, exclusion, income, transfer, and development rights constrained by law, maintenance, financing, and occupancy.
- Insurance/investment wrappers: distinguish insured contingency, underlying investment, fees, exit rules, and lending facility. Borrowing access creates a liability and is not retained cash or guaranteed liquidity.

## Evidence and Communication

Use the user's language. Preserve causal depth without forcing terminology or five-part lectures. Facts, contract terms, calculations, scenarios, and psychological hypotheses must remain distinguishable. Retrieve current rates, laws, prices, and product terms when needed; use tools for arithmetic. General conceptual questions need not become market research.

Do not save temporary balances or inferred motives as durable user facts. Maintain this method from demonstrated gaps, not a past investment conclusion.

## Verification and Transfer Checks

Before completing analysis, confirm relevant checks:
- Rights and underlying assets, ownership and control, and value creation and capture are distinct.
- Exposure has a transmission mechanism; net exposure and payment constraints are considered where material.
- Asset quality, purchase price, and holder-specific value are separate.
- Motives are sourced or qualified; emotional value is not dismissed or automatically called bias.
- Future behavioral effects are conditional and observable, not asserted diagnoses.
- The real alternative, missing evidence, and user's authority remain visible.

Test transfer through contrasting cases:
1. Minority stock: residual rights, delegated capital allocation, dilution, and price; distinguish a valid thesis from break-even anchoring without diagnosing from cost basis alone.
2. Leveraged long bond: linked contracts' currency, repricing, maturity, collateral, and liquidity; positive carry is not arbitrage and apparent safety is not verified safety.
3. Friend's insurance proposal: separate economic terms, protection, service and trust, relationship value, and possible social pressure; do not assume friendship invalidates the offer.
4. Cash awaiting investment: distinguish optionality and reserves from avoidance or indefinite waiting; lower marginal cash utility does not establish what to buy.
5. Same forecasts, different deadlines: explain different marginal values and useful exchange without inventing opposing beliefs.

A good result helps the user see and choose more deliberately; it does not merely produce a bias label or a maximum-return answer.
